Privacy

Last updated 2 November 2026

Life Island is an interactive 3D portfolio. It is run by Syed Abbas Ali, sole trader trading as DigiScalability, ABN 51 256 011 991, Mordialloc VIC 3195, Australia. I'm the only person behind it, so "I" on this page means me. This page says plainly what the island stores, what leaves your device, who receives it and how long it is kept.

The short version

  • No ads, no advertising cookies, no ad networks, and nothing here is sold or shared with data brokers.
  • There are no accounts. Firebase gives your browser a random anonymous ID, so the island never knows who you are unless you tell it.
  • What you type to a villager is sent to Anthropic's Claude model to write the reply. The island itself does not keep what you typed.
  • The voice call in the Contact district streams your microphone live to ElevenLabs, only while the call is on.
  • Messages you send me through the island are stored and emailed to me.
  • Your display name, guestbook notes and race times are visible to other visitors.

What stays in your browser

Your display name, settings (sound, reduced motion, voice on or off, live weather on or off), game progress (coins, hats, tools, items, character colours, race bests) and a few "already shown" flags for hints are kept in your browser's local storage. Firebase keeps your anonymous ID in your browser's IndexedDB. When you arrive, the island also notes in session storage the page that sent you and any utm_source, utm_medium or utm_campaign tags in the link; that note is deleted when you close the tab, and it only leaves your device if you send me a message (see below).

Your character and other visitors

The island's database is Google Firebase (Realtime Database and Cloud Functions, in Google's us-central1 region in the United States). Firebase Anonymous Authentication creates the random ID; it is not linked to an email, an account or your identity.

Names are filtered for offensive words. Please don't put anything in the name field you wouldn't want a stranger to read.

Chat and voice clips with other visitors

Text messages (up to 120 characters) and push-to-talk voice clips go through Firebase to the other visitors on the island and are shown to you based on how near they are. Your microphone is only on while you hold the talk button, and an on-screen indicator shows it. Your browser deletes each message about 12 seconds after sending it, and a scheduled server clean-up removes anything that was left behind. Messages are not logged.

The guestbook

Guestbook notes are public: your display name and note (up to 200 characters) can be read by everyone on the island. Notes are filtered for offensive words. The day's new notes also appear in a private nightly report emailed to me. Notes are kept until I remove them; email me to have yours removed. If you use "Say hi" and add an email address, your note is sent to me privately as a message instead of going into the guestbook.

Messages you send me (the contact form and "Say hi")

The contact form asks for your name (optional), your email address and a message (up to 1,000 characters). "Say hi" with an email address works the same way. Along with what you type, the island saves:

  • your anonymous ID and the time you sent it;
  • the page that sent you to the island (up to 200 characters of its address) and when you arrived;
  • any utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign tags in the link you followed;
  • how many seconds you had been on the island when you sent it.

It is stored in the Firebase database, where other visitors can't read it. A Cloud Function then emails me your name, email address, message and that visit context through my email provider. If more than 10 messages arrive in an hour, the email is skipped and the message waits in the database for me to read. Messages are kept until I delete them; there is no automatic expiry. The nightly report only counts them. I use your address to reply to you and for nothing else.

Talking to the villagers (AI chat)

When you type to a villager (or speak to one, see below), your message, cut to 300 characters, goes to a Firebase Cloud Function. The function checks it against a list of slurs; if one matches, the message goes no further and the villager answers with a scripted line. Otherwise the message is sent to Anthropic's Claude Haiku 4.5 model, together with the villager's character instructions, and the reply is checked again before it is shown. Opening a chat also asks the model to continue the villager's greeting; that request contains nothing you wrote, only game details such as the hat you're wearing and how many days it has been since your last visit.

The island does not store what you type. It keeps the villager's reply so it can be spoken aloud: the reply can only be used for 15 minutes, and a clean-up that runs every 6 hours then deletes it. It also counts calls and tokens (with no text) to stay within a monthly budget. Anthropic processes the message under its commercial terms and privacy policy. Please don't type personal details into a villager chat.

Villager voices

A villager's reply can be read aloud by an ElevenLabs voice: the reply text (never what you typed) is sent to ElevenLabs, and the audio is cached in Firebase so a repeated line isn't paid for twice. The 6-hourly clean-up deletes cached audio once it is more than 7 days old. If the cloud voice isn't available, your browser's built-in voice reads the line on your device.

Speaking instead of typing

If you use the microphone button in a villager chat, your browser's own speech recognition turns your speech into text. Some browsers, such as Chrome, may send the audio to their maker's servers to do this. The island only receives the text, which is then handled like a typed message.

The voice call with my AI receptionist

The Contact district has a "Start voice call" button. Nothing happens until you press it and allow microphone access. A Cloud Function then issues a short-lived link, and your browser connects directly to ElevenLabs: your microphone audio streams live to ElevenLabs for as long as the call is on, ElevenLabs' AI agent turns it into text, and it answers by voice. The transcript is shown on screen. The call ends when you hang up or close the panel. ElevenLabs processes the audio and transcript under its own terms and privacy policy and may keep a record of the conversation.

Bot protection: Firebase App Check and Google reCAPTCHA

When the island connects to its database, shortly after it loads, Firebase App Check uses Google reCAPTCHA Enterprise in the background to check that requests come from the real site and not from a script. reCAPTCHA collects information about your device and browser and how you use the page, and sends it to Google. Google's Privacy Policy and Terms of Service apply.

Rate limits keyed by IP address

Anonymous IDs are free to create, so to stop abuse and cap costs the villager chat, the villager voices and the voice call count requests per IP address instead. Each counter stores your IP address, a request count and the time its window started. A scheduled clean-up deletes counters more than 24 hours old; it runs every 6 hours. The nightly report only counts how many addresses used the chat.

Visit statistics: Vercel Web Analytics

The site is hosted by Vercel and uses Vercel Web Analytics, which counts visits without cookies. For each page view Vercel records the page, the referring site, any UTM tags, and your country, browser, operating system and device type. The island also sends named events such as "world loaded", a district or panel opening, a race or quest finishing, a link being clicked (only the destination site's name), the voice call starting or failing, and how many seconds the visit lasted. Events carry short labels and numbers only, never anything you type. When the island fails to start or hits an error, it also sends a short technical description of the problem (at most two errors per visit).

Live weather (off by default)

The island can mirror your real local weather. This is off unless you turn it on. Until you do, the island uses your device's clock for day and night, which never leaves your device, and a default clear sky. If you switch live weather on:

  • Your browser asks permission for your precise location. If you allow it, the coordinates are sent to Open-Meteo for the forecast and to BigDataCloud to turn them into a place name.
  • If you decline, or it is unavailable, your IP address is sent to ipapi.co for an approximate city-level location, which is then used for the forecast.

The island doesn't store the coordinates. You can switch live weather off again from the weather badge.

Other services your browser talks to

  • Google Fonts serves the island's two display fonts, so Google sees your IP address when they load.
  • The wall screen in the beach house plays a video or page you choose (a YouTube video by default) in an embedded frame. That site may set its own cookies, and its privacy policy applies. Nothing loads until you pick something.
  • Links to LinkedIn, GitHub, RankPilot and other sites take you to those sites, under their own policies.
  • Server logs: Vercel, which hosts the site, and Google Cloud, which runs the database and functions, keep standard request logs (such as IP address, browser and time) for security and operations.

Several of these services are based outside Australia, mainly in the United States, so your data may be processed there.
